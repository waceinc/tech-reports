## Abstract

A PLC ladder is often checked against a plant model before it meets equipment, and the verdict depends on that model. We asked how often the verdict of the same ladder changes when only the way parts are moved is changed, in two simulated conveyor cells: a shuttle cell (cell A) and a sort cell with a pusher and an inclined chute (cell B(3i)). Condition F moves parts with fast kinematic rules; condition M0 computes the same parts as rigid boxes in MuJoCo, with the same actuators, sensors and ladder; condition M1 is M0 with three sort-cell ladder constants re-derived from MuJoCo by a rule fixed before calibration. Before any official run we registered eight predictions and three falsification conditions. The official round had 607 runs, all valid; the 115 pairs that were run twice (9 fast-model gate pairs and 106 MuJoCo pairs) were identical. In the sort cell, the correct ladder passed under F in all 12 batches of the `normal` scenario and failed under M0 in 4, all four in the batches with five defective parts (first lock: chute full in 3, chute confirm window in 1). The same 4 failed under M1, now all with chute full, so the re-derived constants did not remove any of them. In the 13 `curtain` and `recovery` checks of the correct ladder, F and M0 agreed. Detection of known defects by the expected reason did not change between F and M0 (21 of 28 pairs in both, no discordant pair); the 7 misses were the same 7 batches in all conditions and, by the unreleased tick records, come from the scenario's trigger, not from the plant model. **All eight predictions held and no falsification condition was triggered.** Two of them say less than the count suggests: P3 held largely by construction, because the re-derivation does not change the chute capacity on which a chute-full lock depends, and P8 counted as holding two settings that made every batch fail, including the eight that agree at baseline. The result describes two simulator models of the same cells on one machine and one build; it does not say which model is closer to a physical conveyor, and no physical PLC or conveyor was compared. The reversals are sensitive to assumed physical values: lowering friction by 30 % removed all four, and raising it by 30 % made all 12 batches fail. The sort cell here is the 3i design and ladder, not the 3k cell of TR-2026-03 and TR-2026-04 (TR-2026-02 ran cell A only), so its results cannot be read against those reports. The application source is not public; the release bundle holds the pre-registration with its amendment and corrections, the public result files and the scripts that recompute every verdict.

## 1. Question

A ladder that drives a simulated plant is judged by what the plant model makes happen: which sensors switch when, whether parts reach the right exit, whether the ladder locks out. The model that moves the parts is therefore part of the test. We asked, before any official run, with the same cell definition, the same ladder and the same stimuli:

1. **Defect detection.** Of the ladders with a known injected defect (negative ladders), how many does each model detect for the expected reason, and how many pairs disagree?
2. **Correct-ladder reversals.** How often does the verdict of the correct ladder ([PASS] or [FAIL]) change with the model, in which direction, and what is the first event that differs?
3. **After re-derivation.** Does a reversal remain when the correct-ladder constants that were originally fitted to the fast model's history are re-derived from MuJoCo (condition M1)?

"The MuJoCo model" is a bundle, not only the contact solver: MuJoCo rigid-body contact, the transport rules that carry fast-model rules over to it (Section 3.2), the assumed physical values (Appendix B), and the absence of some fast-model rules (Appendix C). The confirmatory claim is that *this bundle* changes verdicts; splitting a reversal into "physics" and "port, rule or bookkeeping differences" is done only by the pre-registered classification and mapping (Section 5.1), not as a confirmed claim. **Which model is right is not decided.** There is no physical cell, so the report does not claim that MuJoCo is more realistic.

## 2. Why this is a simulation-only question

The same ladder, stimuli and part placement can be run side by side with only the transport model changed; the actuators (pusher cylinder trajectory, belt motor, sensor logic) use the same code in both models, and runs within one build were bit-reproducible in this round (P6). The comparison between models takes place inside one simulator on one machine. Absolute verdicts depend on the cell definitions and on assumed physical values and do not describe any site. The report says nothing about physical PLCs, conveyors, sorters or boxes.

### 3.2 The MuJoCo part-transport rules and model (disclosure)

This section and Appendices B and C describe the transport rules and model in enough detail to serve as their disclosure (see Licence).

- **Mixed plant.** Actuators stay kinematic and come from the same core as the fast model; only part transport and contact are computed in MuJoCo. The pusher plate is a free dynamic box whose position and velocity are overwritten at every 1 ms boundary from the core cylinder trajectory (qpos = core face position, qvel = next face minus this face over 1 ms), so the contact solver sees the plate's velocity; a kinematic (mocap) plate alone let the plate sink centimetres into a part during development. Deck surface velocity is set every 1 ms from the core motor's belt samples.
- **Transport rule v1 (cell A).** The part-drop rule is the fast core's rule: the first 1 ms sample with |x| > L/2 + 1e-12 raises `PART_DROP` (a secondary rule records a side drop when the centre falls below the deck). A part whose route is no longer 0 is frozen (inactive, no collision). Floor top at y = 0. Automatic reset off; warnings throw. One part. Tilt and yaw of parts over the deck are reported, with a flag above 2°.
- **Transport rule v4 (cell B(3i)).** Same principles for 20 parts, plus: (a) **chute x force** — every 1 ms each chute part that is not held gets an x-only force F = m·g·(μx − μiso·cos θ·|vx|/|v|), clamped to [0, m·|vx|/1 ms], opposite to vx, where μx is the fast model's chute x friction (0.85), μiso and θ are the isotropic friction and the local slope of the arc or chute, so that the total x deceleration follows the fast rule while MuJoCo's isotropic friction supplies the rest (the force acts at the centre of mass, so the yaw damping torque of real anisotropic friction is absent); (b) **`CHUTE-HOLD`** — while the chute-jam fault is on, every chute part is held at its pose; (c) **chute capture** (route 1) by the same criterion as the fast model, at the tick end; (d) **output arrival** (route 2) when the contact stop position is within 2 mm of the fast model's output cap — used only for route counting and the next part's cap, never as a PLC sensor input; (e) **CENTER-SNAP** evaluated on the deepest footprint corner (yaw included) with a 2 mm contact tolerance (measured maximum penetration 1.66 mm); (f) a collected part stays a chute obstacle until it has moved clear, then becomes inactive; (g) an initial contact of a part with anything but a deck throws at load. v4 adds to v3 two sensitivity switches (arc friction scale and chute x force on/off) and the rotated-sensor-footprint switch; their defaults leave the v3 behaviour unchanged.
- **Two hashes.** `values_sha256` covers the rule string, the named physics values, the full compiled scene (MJCF), the compiled options and the engine version, and in cell B every plant parameter (so it differs per seed, belt factor and initial lateral position). `setup_sha256` covers only the rule string, the values, the compiled options and the engine version, so it is one registered value per cell and physics setting. The runner checks both against the pre-registered values in every M run. The scene MJCF is produced by the unreleased transport code; its hash is released, its text is not, so a reader can compare the hashes in the result files with the seed table but cannot recompute them.
- **Reports that no verdict reads.** Per-part pose, velocity, support surface and gap, stacked and on-floor flags every tick; the first plate-to-box contact sample of each pushed box (M from the contact list; F, behind a flag that does not change any other byte of the F response).

Appendix B lists the 15 (cell A) and 32 (cell B(3i)) named physics values with their defaults and sources; Appendix C lists every difference between the two models, row by row.

### 3.3 Cells and ladders, and why cell B(3i) is not the cell B of TR-2026-03 and TR-2026-04

- **Cell A** (shuttle, one part, PLC scan 10 ms, 5 scans per plant tick): correct ladder (27 rungs, SHA-256 `5058bd0b…`) and two negative ladders, `late-reverse` (reversal 150 scans late) and `swapped-sensors` (left and right sensors swapped), both expected to cause `PART_DROP`. The correct ladder is the file used in TR-2026-02 to TR-2026-04; the two negative ladders are the files released with TR-2026-02.
- **Cell B(3i)** (sort, 20 parts, defect rate 0.2, PLC scan 10 ms, one scan per tick): the earlier pusher-assembly design (device definition 1.5.0) with its **3i ladder** (788 rungs, `eb44ce59…`) and three negative ladders, `push-timing-off` (firing +50 scans; expected a push-timing event), `ignore-curtain` (expected the bridge judgment `PLC_CURTAIN_IGNORED`) and `no-sort-check` (no defect-route check; no expected reason exists anywhere in the 3i bridge, so it counts only in the secondary metric).
- **Not comparable with TR-2026-03 and TR-2026-04.** What those reports call cell B is the later 3k cell (device definition 1.6.0 with a position rule on an accumulating high-speed counter, correct ladder of 569 rungs, `05999e23…`), run on macOS; TR-2026-02 dropped cell B before its runs and reports cell A only (the pre-registration's §11 names TR-2026-02 among these reports; it has no cell B results). Cell B(3i) differs in cell design, correct ladder, negative-ladder set and platform (Linux). The MuJoCo mode refuses the 3k design. **The cell B(3i) results of this report must not be pooled with, or read side by side with, the cell B results of TR-2026-03 and TR-2026-04, and they neither correct nor support those reports' conclusions.** Same seed numbers do not mean the same batches. Only the cell A ladder files are shared; even there the platform differs, so cell A results are compared with the earlier reports at the level of verdicts only.

### 3.4 Conditions and the re-derivation rule (M1)

| Condition | Plant | Ladder constants | Reference logic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **F** | fast | original | original |
| **M0** | MuJoCo, default values | original | original |
| **M1** | as M0 | sort-cell correct ladder and the three negative ladders regenerated with re-derived constants | original logic with the new constants |

The 3i constants record their own provenance; three are recorded as fitted to the fast model's history (chute confirm window 165 to 235 scans, chute full ≥ 45 scans, and the subtraction 7 in the firing delay ⌊PulseSpan × 232 / (i × 50)⌋ − 7). The end-to-end fitting procedure is not documented anywhere, so the pre-registration defines its own rule: on 6 calibration batches, record the F closed-loop run of the correct ladder, replay its requests open-loop on M0, measure the same statistic on both with the original ladder engine, and set the new constant by a fixed formula. The rule ran once, after the freeze (Section 6):

| Constant | Statistic (6 calibration batches, 24 defective parts) | New value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chute confirm window 165–235 | age at filtered chute rise: F [195, 196], M [197, 234]; margins kept 30 and 39; median shift +38 scans | **167–273** |
| Chute full 45 | longest passing sensor-on interval: F 11, M 12 scans | **⌈45 × 12/11⌉ = 50** |
| Firing-delay subtraction 7 | first plate contact position: median F −0.20420 m, M −0.20902 m, Δx −0.004818 m | **7 − ⌈0.004818 / 0.012⌉ = 6** |
| Output window 198–269, arrival-on limit 29, output full 100 | F and M 233–234; 19; 19 — not near the limits | unchanged (not re-derived by rule) |

No calibration seed was skipped. The four M1 ladders differ from the originals only in constant literals (36, 36, 36 and 33 lines). M1 does not change the chute's capacity (sensor position and queue length), which matters for P3 (Section 4). Cell A constants have no recorded source and were not re-derived, so cell A M1 equals M0 and was not run.

### 3.5 Inputs

- **Cell A:** no seed; scenarios `normal` (1,000 ticks) and `cycle-stop` (1,200 ticks, stop button at tick 600). 3 ladders × 2 scenarios.
- **Cell B(3i):** official seeds from SHA-256("TR-2026-05/official/" + k), first 4 bytes, k = 1, 2, …, skipping seeds 0 to 3; defect counts read by loading only (no tick run); the first 4 seeds with 3, 4 and 5 defective parts each — **12 batches**, drawn after the freeze from the committed rule (seed table `d02d62f3…`; the official set needed k = 1 to 35, and 52 values of k were examined across the official, calibration and development sets). Batches with 6 or more defective parts were excluded because the fast model's chute queue can overflow there too. Lateral start position 0.
- **Pairs:** correct ladder × `normal` (5,000 ticks) × 12, × `curtain` (1,000 ticks; operator enters from the tick after the first pusher command) × 12, × `recovery` (2,800 ticks) × 1 (first 5-defect batch); `push-timing-off` × `normal` (1,800) × 12; `ignore-curtain` × `curtain` (500) × 12; `no-sort-check` × `flow` (800) × 1. Main runs: F 56, M0 56, M1 50 = 162.

### 3.6 Verdicts and measures

- **Process verdict [PASS]** = complete ∧ no physical event (production events excluded) ∧ no bridge judgment. Cell A complete = visits right, left × 3, `Rounds == 3`, all outputs off at the end. Cell B complete = every defective part at route 1 or 3, every good part at route 2, `Arrivals == 20`.
- **Correct `curtain`**: safety verdict (curtain detected; belt running the tick before; belt and pusher commands off on every curtain tick; stop within 0.265 s; no physical event). **Correct `recovery`**: the bridge's recovery check.
- **Ledger-free verdict** (reported only): as the process verdict, but a good part at route 0 counts as delivered if it is past the output sensor (x > 2.2 m) and not on the floor.
- **Reversal classes** (pre-registered): (a) ladder lock (FaultCode), (b) physical event or bridge judgment, (c) completion items only, (d) other. Reversals for P1, P2, P3, P8, F1, F3 count (a)+(b)+(d). The first differing event is mapped in advance to a row of the difference table (Appendix C).
- **Expected-reason detection** (main metric, 28 pairs: cell A 4, `push-timing-off` 12, `ignore-curtain` 12) = negative ladder [FAIL] ∧ expected reason present. **Verdict detection** (secondary, 29 pairs incl. `no-sort-check`), split into the 13 pairs that fail by run length alone and the other 16.
- **Reference match:** the correct ladder's outputs must equal a reference logic scan by scan up to the first lock (one documented one-scan shape excepted); any other difference invalidates the round.

### 3.7 Runs, invalidation and positive controls

One Ubuntu machine (Linux aarch64, NVIDIA GB10, Ubuntu 24.04.5, Clang 21.1.8, Release, `-ffp-contract=off`), one executable (`d58c3928…`), libmujoco `ddc46a81…`, Node v24.21.0; runs sequential. The round runner refuses to start if the fixed-input file (`fixed.json`) or any file it lists differs from its SHA-256. Run categories: `[VALID]`, `[PLANT_ERROR]` (MuJoCo warning; not a verdict), `[INVALID]`. Every M pair runs twice and the full trace hash is compared; a differing pair would go to a 10-run majority rule. The round is invalid if the F determinism gate (9 pairs, two runs each) differs, if a reference-match difference occurs before the first lock, if more than 10 % of an M condition's runs are `[PLANT_ERROR]`, if a planned run is missing, or if a mode check fails. Further runs per round: 14 open-loop replays (F requests sent to M0 without a ladder), 156 P8 sensitivity runs, 10 × (f + 2) model-assumption runs (f = number of M0 reversals in correct `normal`), and 100 runs of the 25 cell-A interlock-removal variants of TR-2026-03 (E4).

Before the official round, positive control 1 (the fast model reproduces the macOS 3i reference results item by item: 30 of 31 checks, the remaining one being the `no-sort-check` "detection" item that no 3i ladder can pass; the gate file's overall status is [FAIL] for that item, which the macOS reference fails too; the pre-registered criterion is "no-sort-check [FAIL]", which was met) and positive control 2 (3 development seeds through runner, tables, independent recompute and public conversion) were run. The first positive control 2 was invalid (Section 6). The regression tests counted for positive control 1 were an existing record (11 of 11) of the same executable, not a fresh run.

### 3.8 What was seen before registration

Before the freeze, development runs used seeds 1 to 3 only. They had shown: the cell A correct ladder [PASS] under F and M0 in both `normal` and `cycle-stop`; the cell B correct ladder with seed 1 (5 defective parts) [PASS] under F and [FAIL] under M0, first lock chute full at tick 1710, with boxes yawing on the chute and the fifth defective box stopping inside the chute sensor band; open-loop sensor edges within 10 ms on the belt and the chute sensor later under M (mean +164 ms). P1 and P7 are reproduction predictions from these observations. **Because cell A has no seed, its official input equals the development input; its correct-ladder results are therefore exploratory (E6) and carry no prediction.** The reference-match invalidation range, the exclusion of `no-sort-check` from the main metric, the defect-count bins and the sensitivity items were set after seeing these runs.

## 7. Threats to validity and what this report does not claim

- This is a simulation. Neither model was compared with a physical cell, PLC, conveyor, sorter or box. **The report does not claim that the MuJoCo model is more correct**, only that changing this bundle changed these verdicts.
- [PASS], [FAIL] and "detection" are values of the verdict rules inside the simulator. They do not say that a ladder is correct or safe on equipment, and no plant-connected test in either model replaces commissioning. Detection counts are for the five negative ladders chosen here and describe no product.
- One machine, one build, one MuJoCo library. Determinism and the comparison are claimed only there.
- **Cell B(3i) is not the cell B of TR-2026-03 and TR-2026-04** (Section 3.3). Nothing here says how sensitive the 3k cell's verdicts are to the plant model.
- **Cell A correct-ladder results are exploratory**: the identical input had been run under M0 before registration, and its official outcome was also seen in the invalid first round before the second round.
- All four reversals are in the five-defect bin, and the bins are a design choice (12 batches drawn by a seed rule and binned by defect count), not a sample of a defect rate of 0.2. No confidence interval is given.
- The reversals are sensitive to assumed physical values (Section 5.4): ±30 % changes of all sliding friction either remove all of them or make every batch fail. Two of the four disappear at half the time step. The MuJoCo-side values (guide and wall geometry, zero wall friction, static-friction weight, the two 2 mm tolerances, facet count) were assumed, not measured; the 2 mm tolerances were not varied.
- Sensors use an axis-aligned box at the part centre; rotated parts are not seen as rotated except in one setting. The chute x force acts at the centre of mass and lacks the yaw damping of real anisotropic friction, which tends to increase yaw on the chute. The eight P8 settings do not include the most direct causes of yaw (sensor footprint, plate friction, deck kinetic friction); the footprint is varied only among the model-assumption settings (Section 5.5) and plate friction not at all.
- `recovery` and `no-sort-check` ran on one five-defect batch only, so under M they can overlap with a chute-full lock.
- CENTER-ENTRY cannot occur in M, and no positive case of CENTER-SNAP was tested in either model.
- M1 is this study's re-derivation rule, not a reproduction of an original fitting procedure (none is documented); it does not change chute capacity, so P3 was expected to hold by construction.
- Actuators are kinematic in both models (no cylinder force or stiffness limit, no plate dynamics), so stroke-time faults cannot differ between models.
- MuJoCo's solver reaching its iteration limit without convergence raises no warning, so it would not appear as `[PLANT_ERROR]`.
- The 7 missed `ignore-curtain` detections and the one failed correct `curtain` run come from the scenario trigger (no pusher command in time), identically in every condition.
- E2 (in full), E3 and the per-tag edge statistics were planned but not computed by the fixed scripts.
- The independent recompute recounts the same per-run files with separately written code; it does not measure anything by another method. It was itself changed after the results (correction 2).
- The original methodology review of 2026-09-29 that the pre-registration summarises could not be found on the machine used; the pre-registration says so.
